# Strategic Rationale

Why this project exists, what it competes against, and when to stop. Updated for stage-gated structure.

This is the document you read when you wonder if you should be doing applications instead.

---

## 1. The honest framing

This project is **partly passion, partly portable signal**. Both are real. The stage-gated structure exists so you do not over-invest before knowing whether the marginal hour pays.

**Passion component:** modeling, FX as new domain, XGBoost depth, LP as econometric extension.

**Portable signal:** walk-forward CV, three-pass tuning, permutation importance, SHAP, regime analysis, LP-IV familiarity. All signal to multiple lanes (FX research, credit/model validation, applied econ, data science).

**What this is not:** the most direct Fitch signal (that is IFRS9 and GARCH-EVT), and not a trading model.

## 2. Opportunity cost (revised for stage-gated)

| Stop point | Cumulative hours | Opportunity cost equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| End of Stage 1 | 8-10h | 8-10 cover letters via P11A |
| End of Stage 2 | 14-18h | 14-18 cover letters or 8 coffee chats |
| End of Stage 3 | 26-33h | 26-33 cover letters; finishing GARCH-EVT instead |
| End of Stage 4 | 36-45h | 36-45 cover letters; ~25% of total job-search bandwidth for the period |

Stage gates let you stop exactly where the marginal hour stops paying.

## 3. Time allocation rule

Cap project time at **5-8h per week**. Project gets zero in weeks with high application or interview load. No guilt.

Illustrative weekly split when interviews are not active:

- 18-22h applications + cover letters + networking + interview prep
- 6-8h this project
- 4-6h GARCH-EVT and IFRS9 wrap-up
- Rest: life

If actual time available is less, this project shrinks first.

## 4. Stage-by-stage stop criteria

Each gate has explicit stop triggers.

### After Stage 1

**Stop and ship if:** 
- The descriptive analysis tells a clean story (BoC three factors visibly present in charts; rolling correlation instability is the story).
- You realize the forecasting layer is not the most valuable thing for your current job pipeline.

**Continue to Stage 2 if:**
- You have a clear hypothesis about which factor block carries forecasting signal.
- You have weekly project capacity for another 6-8h within the next 2-3 weeks.

### After Stage 2

**Stop and ship if:**
- Random walk dominates obviously. The Meese-Rogoff confirmation is the story; XGBoost won't change it.
- Ridge coefficients align with macro priors interestingly; that alone is a portfolio-worthy interpretation result.

**Continue to Stage 3 if:**
- Ridge shows non-trivial OOS R² and you want to test whether nonlinearity adds.
- You actively want to learn XGBoost deeply (the learning-artifact value, not just the forecasting value).

### After Stage 3

**Stop and ship if:**
- Project hours are at 30+ and applications need attention.
- Stage 3 results are already publishable as a complete forecasting and interpretation study.
- You decide LP is not the right next learning investment (other priorities: refresh econometrics for a specific role).

**Continue to Stage 4 if:**
- You want the econometric-rigor signal for macro/applied econ roles.
- Stage 3 surfaced an interesting feature whose dynamic response you want to study.
- You have 8-12h of further project capacity.

## 5. Hard kill criteria (any stage)

Stop the project entirely (or downgrade) if:

- You receive a Fitch offer or another strong offer and acceptance is imminent. Project pivots from job-search to skill-deepening, lower priority.
- After 20h cumulative, you cannot pitch the project in 90 seconds. Framing is wrong; rewrite before continuing.
- Application volume drops below 12h/week for two consecutive weeks because of this project. Reallocate.
- A phase gate produces a clearly broken result you cannot diagnose. Halt and ask for review.

## 6. The passion-vs-prospects misframing

The dichotomy is partly false. Real passion craft with portable skill is also job-signal craft, IF the skills are portable and the lane is broad. This project's skills are portable. The lane is broad if you complete at least Stages 1-3.

The dichotomy becomes real only when:

- Hours spent on craft displace application volume disproportionately.
- The project's scope drifts toward narrow specialty signal.
- Over-polish replaces shipping.

Mitigations are structural, not motivational:

- Weekly time cap (8h)
- Stage gates with ship triggers
- Decision rules at each gate
- Hard kill criteria

## 7. What this project should change in self-concept

Before: "MA student building portfolio for econ/risk/markets roles."

After: "Applied empirical economist who has built and shipped projects in macro-finance, credit, tail risk, and forecasting with disciplined ML and econometric validation."

That shift is the ROI regardless of which specific offer lands first.

## 8. What this project should NOT change

It should not change application volume. It should not change the priority of HKMFE, GARCH-EVT, IFRS9 in the broader portfolio. It should not become the project mentioned at every coffee chat (rotate per audience).

It should also not become a substitute for actually applying. The portfolio is foundation; applications, referrals, and conversations are the actual job-search work.

## 9. Concrete commitments

Paste into your weekly control file:

> I will cap this project at 8 hours per week. If applications or interviews require more time in a given week, the project gets zero that week. At each stage gate I will decide explicitly to ship or continue, not drift. If at any point I cannot pitch the current state in 90 seconds, I stop and rewrite the framing before continuing.

That commitment is the difference between a portfolio piece and a sinkhole.

## 10. Final note

The act of asking "should I do passion or job prospects?" is itself healthy. The wrong answers are (a) abandon entirely, (b) over-invest by ignoring cost, (c) deny the tension. The right answer is to do it, but pace it explicitly with ship gates. The four stages are exactly that apparatus.

Now go finish Stage 1.
